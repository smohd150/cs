What is a graphics card?

Lesson 1: functions of a graphics card

  • Hardware responsible for quickly and clearly producing the images you see on your monitor
  • The interface between a computer and a monitor
  • Also known as a graphics adapter (and often called a GPU)

Parts of a graphics card

Four key components

Graphics card interfaceThe connectorThe PCIe (peripheral component interconnect express) interface is the physical connector that attaches the card to the motherboard. The motherboard has a PCIe x16 slot for it.
GPU (graphics processing unit)The artistA chip or electronic circuit that renders the graphics.
Video memoryThe sketchbookStores all data related to images or graphics.
Display outputThe cable portConnects to a monitor and allows information exchange.

How a graphics card works

From CPU instruction to picture on your screen. 6 steps.

1

CPU sends instructions

The CPU gives image instructions to the graphics card as binary data, passing through the graphics card interface.

2

Interpret and make a wire frame

The card interprets the instructions and converts the data into signals. It creates a wire frame out of straight lines.

3

Rasterize

The image is rasterized: rasterization fills in the remaining pixels.

4

Add lighting, texture and color

The GPU adds lighting, texture and color to the image.

5

Video memory holds the result

As the GPU creates images, video memory holds the completed image and all its data (pixel, color and location on the screen).

6

Send to the monitor

Digital data from video memory goes to the monitor as signals. The monitor interprets them and renders them as images.

Types of graphics cards

Lesson 2: Why replace a graphics card?

They also use enormous amounts of power

A graphics card is key to brighter, clearer images and better display performance. Reasons to replace one:

  • It is malfunctioning
  • To improve display visualization
  • To improve the desired resolution and frame performance

Most new computers come with a graphics card, but over time you may upgrade for a higher-quality picture.

Removing a graphics card

7 steps

1

Remove the driver

Uninstall the graphics driver from the computer.

2

Power off and unplug

Switch off your computer and unplug it from power.

3

Open the side panel

Open the side panel of the computer and find the graphics card.

4

Unplug the power cable

Unplug the graphics card's power cable.

5

Unscrew the card

Remove the screws holding the card in place.

6

Release the clip or tab

Push the clip or tab to release the card.

7

Pull it out

Pull the card out and place it into an antistatic bag.

Installing a graphics card

6 steps. Basically the reverse of removal, ending with the driver.

1

Align the card

Remove the new card from its antistatic bag and align the PCIe component to an empty slot on the motherboard.

2

Secure it

Secure the GPU with screws.

3

Connect power

If the card needs separate power, install the power cable into the card. If the cable's pins differ from the motherboard's pin slots, consider a power adapter.

4

Verify all connections

5

Power on

6

Install the driver

Lesson 3: Warning signs of a failing card

What to look for

A powerful card renders images quickly and clearly. If it begins to fail you can install, uninstall or upgrade it, then update the drivers to get the new card's best performance.

  • Artifacts (odd visual glitches on screen)
  • Frozen screen or stuttering display
  • Screen glitches
  • Loud fan noise
  • System crash

Troubleshooting a graphics card

Trial and error: eliminate the possible causes one by one

Things to check:

  • The driver
  • The power supply
  • Disable the sound card
  • Slow down the AGP
  • Let an overheated card cool

The AGP (accelerated graphics port) is the high-speed port a graphics card attaches to.

1. Check the driver

Problem: the driver is incompatible or malfunctioning and causing crashes.
Fix: update the correct driver from the manufacturer's website.

3. Disable the sound card

Problem: sound hardware can hit bugs when playing graphics-rich content.
Fix: disable it and see if the graphics problem goes away.

4. Slow down the AGP

Problem: inconsistent data transfer causes stutters, freezes and data corruption.
Fix: use configuration settings to reduce the AGP speed for more stability.

2. Check the power supply

Power supply issues can directly impact or damage the card.

Low power supply

Constant low power makes the card work harder and overheat.

Overclocking

Increasing the processor clock speed above its designated speed.

Power surges

Surges and blackouts give an abnormal power supply.

Fix: check that the power voltage is correct for the card and use an uninterruptible power supply (UPS) to regulate the power input.

5. Let an overheated card cool

Unexpected glitches, stuttering and crashes in graphics-rich apps may mean internal components are generating a lot of heat.

Thermal compound

Absorbs heat and allows faster cooling.

Clean

Use compressed air to remove dust buildup.

Shut down

Shut the system down to let overheated components cool.

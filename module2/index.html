Why safety matters

Lesson 1: safety best practices

When a computer has issues, you may need to check the hardware directly. Whenever you do, following precautions and safety measures is extremely important — for you and for the parts.

Protecting yourself

Before you touch anything

  • Do not wear clothes that can gather static
  • Avoid metallic ornaments (rings, bracelets, watches)
  • Keep your workspace tidy and well lit

Protecting the computer parts

ESD — electrostatic discharge — is the enemy

Never touch internal components while the power cord is still connected — this risks both electrocution and broken parts.

To protect parts from ESD, ground yourself by:

  • Wearing an antistatic wrist strap
  • Standing on an antistatic mat
  • Touching an unpainted metal object that is in contact with the ground
  • Disconnecting all computer equipment and peripherals first

During removal or installation

Handle everything gently

  • Remove screws, sort them by size, and store them carefully
  • Remove or insert components gently. If a part won't seat easily, something's mismatched — stop and check
  • Store components temporarily in an antistatic bag. Avoid touching gold leads or circuits
  • When reassembling, put covers and screws back in their proper places

After installation: power supply safety

The last step matters too

  • Use the correct wattage power supply — different units produce different wattages, and the wrong one means the computer won't work
  • Make sure all circuits and components are in place before turning on the power
  • If anything heats up abnormally, or smells of burning or smoke, disconnect the power immediately

The technician's toolkit

Three categories, one goal: safe and efficient work

A computer technician needs a working toolkit for installation, repair and troubleshooting.

Safety tools

Protect the equipment from ESD

Antistatic mat

Protects sensitive electronics from static damage. Usually made of PVC. Dissipates ESD, which could otherwise destroy components.

Antistatic wrist strap

Grounds the person handling the hardware. Conductor threads carry the electrical charge safely to ground through a metal clip.

Safety gloves

Cut-resistant gloves the technician should also wear while handling hardware.

Technical tools

For installing and repairing

Precision screwdriver set

Magnetized tips and screw bowls. One set replaces many separate screwdrivers. Used to mount/unmount components.

Flashlight

Checks wiring, finds a misplaced screw, and lights up dark areas like the circuit board.

Tweezers

Picks up tiny parts that fall down a shaft or through wires. An ESD-safe metal tweezer resists magnetic flux.

Pliers (needle nose)

Also called electronic pliers — can even cut components on a circuit board.

Wire cutter

Cuts copper, brass, aluminum or steel wire. Has an insulated handle to prevent shock.

Wire stripper

Opposing blades remove insulation from a wire without damaging the wire inside, so it can connect to another wire.

Crimp

Looks like a plier. Attaches a connector to a cable end by deforming metal, with no melting or fusing needed.

Cable tester

Spot-checks internet connectivity. Detects short circuits, faulty extensions, crossed or exposed wires, and broken connections via an LED indicator.

Toner and probe kit

Identifies a specific cable in a bundle, or an unlabeled port. The toner sends a signal; the probe beeps when it's near the right cable.

Loopback adapter

Also called a loopback plug. Tests a network port or cable by rerouting the signal back to its source, checking output matches input.

Multimeter

Also called a circuit analyzer. Measures voltage, current and resistance with red and black probes. Not required to set up a computer, but useful for repairs.

Cable ties

Bundle and organize cables, inside and outside the computer, preventing tangles.

Cleaning tools

Keep the hardware — and yourself — clean

Compressed air

Blows dirt, dust and debris straight out of hard-to-reach areas, right from the canister.

Lint-free cloth

Doesn't shed fibers, doesn't build up a static charge (ESD-resistant), and won't scratch or smudge. Don't use it right against circuitry. Also carry alcohol-based sanitizer to keep yourself safe.
